# Policy and Risk Notes

This file defines safe vs. risky recommendations for Google Business Profile audits. Every recommendation in a client-facing report must pass the "safe" test.

---

## Safe recommendations

These are always safe to recommend:

### Business name
- Use the real-world business name exactly as it appears on signage, website and trading documents
- Remove any extra keywords, locations, or marketing taglines from the business name field

### Categories
- Choose the single most accurate primary category from Google's category list
- Add 2–4 genuinely relevant secondary categories
- Remove any category that does not describe what the business actually does

### Contact information
- Use the business's real phone number — the one they answer
- Ensure the phone number is consistent across GBP, website, and directories
- Make phone numbers clickable (tel: link) on the website

### Address and service area
- For businesses with a physical location customers visit: show the real address
- For service-area businesses (tradespeople, mobile services): hide the address and set the service area
- Never use a virtual office, PO box, or friend's address unless it is staffed and customers can visit

### Opening hours
- Set normal weekly opening hours accurately
- Set special hours for bank holidays, Christmas, and other closures
- Update hours immediately if they change

### Website link
- Link to the real business website
- Consider linking to a specific relevant page (booking page, menu, contact) if the homepage is weak

### Services / products / menu
- List the real services or products the business offers
- Keep descriptions accurate and up to date
- Remove services that are no longer offered

### Photos
- Upload real photos of the real business
- Include variety: exterior, interior, team, work examples, products
- Add new photos regularly — at least once a month
- Use a modern phone camera; avoid heavy filters or stock photography

### Reviews
- Ask every genuine customer for an honest review — not just the happy ones
- Use the "Ask for reviews" link from GBP to create a short, shareable URL
- Print a QR code that links directly to the review form and display it at the till/reception/on receipts
- Reply to all reviews — positive and negative — within a few days
- Thank positive reviewers sincerely
- Respond to negative reviews calmly, acknowledge the concern, and offer to put it right offline
- Never argue with a reviewer publicly

### Website and customer journey
- Make the primary action (call, book, order, enquire) obvious and one tap away on mobile
- Use large, clear buttons with specific labels — "Call us now", "Book online", "Order takeaway"
- List core services/products with brief descriptions and price ranges where possible
- Test the contact form, booking system, and phone number monthly
- Ensure the website loads quickly on a 4G connection

### Posts and updates
- Post once a month with a photo and a clear action
- Use posts for offers, news, seasonal information, or showcasing recent work

## Risky recommendations — never make these

### Business name manipulation
- ❌ "Add keywords to your business name to rank higher"
- ❌ "Add your town or city to your business name"
- ❌ "Add 'best' or 'top' or 'number one' to your business name"
- ❌ "Use a trading name that includes search keywords"

**Why:** Keyword-stuffing the business name is the most common reason for GBP suspension. Google's guidelines require the real-world business name only.

### Reviews
- ❌ "Buy reviews" or "get reviews from a review service"
- ❌ "Offer a discount or freebie in exchange for a review"
- ❌ "Only ask happy customers for reviews"
- ❌ "Ask customers to mention specific keywords in their review"
- ❌ "Leave reviews for your own business"
- ❌ "Ask staff, friends or family to leave reviews"
- ❌ "Create a second profile to collect more reviews"

**Why:** Fake, incentivised, or biased reviews violate Google's guidelines and UK consumer protection law (Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008). They can result in review removal, profile suspension, or legal consequences.

### Profile manipulation
- ❌ "Create a second Google Business Profile for the same business"
- ❌ "Use a virtual office address to appear in a different town"
- ❌ "Set up profiles in nearby towns you don't actually serve"
- ❌ "Change your category to something popular but inaccurate"
- ❌ "Use a friend's address to look like you are in a better location"

**Why:** Duplicate profiles and fake addresses can lead to permanent suspension of all profiles for that business.

### Claims and guarantees
- ❌ "I can get you to number one on Google"
- ❌ "Guaranteed first-page ranking"
- ❌ "Guaranteed increase in calls/bookings/sales"
- ❌ "Guaranteed improvement in your Google ranking"
- ❌ "My system guarantees more reviews"

**Why:** No one can guarantee Google rankings or customer behaviour. Making these claims is misleading, damages trust, and may violate advertising standards.

### Scraping and unauthorised access
- ❌ "Scrape competitor profiles to see what works"
- ❌ "Access the GBP dashboard without the owner's permission"
- ❌ "Claim or manage a profile without authorisation"
- ❌ "Scrape customer review data for marketing"

**Why:** Unauthorised access to business profiles is a terms-of-service violation and may be illegal.

## What the report must not claim

The client-facing report must never include these claims:

- Guaranteed Google ranking improvements
- "Number 1 on Google" or "first page guarantee"
- Guaranteed increase in calls, bookings, orders or sales
- Guaranteed increase in reviews
- Guaranteed outcomes from any AI or automation tool
- A specific timeframe for ranking improvements
- "Secret" or "hack" or "trick" language
- "Google loves this" or any claim about what Google's algorithm prefers
- Any claim that the recommendations will definitely produce a specific result

Instead, use language like:

- "This is likely to help because …"
- "Most businesses in your situation see improvement by …"
- "A common fix for this is …"
- "Based on the evidence, the biggest opportunity is …"
- "Other local businesses have found that …"

---

## UK-specific notes

- UK consumer law applies to reviews and marketing claims. The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) has taken action against fake reviews.
- The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) can investigate misleading claims in marketing materials, including audit reports.
- If the business collects personal data (e.g. for a mailing list or follow-up emails), UK GDPR applies.
- For limited companies, the registered company name and number should appear on the website.
- For VAT-registered businesses, the VAT number should appear on the website.

---

## Quick test: is this recommendation safe?

Before including a recommendation in a report, ask:

1. Would Google's guidelines allow this? If no, stop.
2. Is this legal under UK consumer and advertising regulations? If no, stop.
3. Am I confident the recommendation is accurate based on the evidence supplied? If no, flag as "needs manual verification" or remove it.
4. Would I be comfortable if the business owner repeated this advice publicly? If no, reword it.
5. Does the recommendation promise a specific outcome? If yes, reword to describe the likely benefit, not a guarantee.
