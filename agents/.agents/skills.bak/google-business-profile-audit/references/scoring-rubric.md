# Scoring Rubric

Scores are directional, not definitive. Use them to prioritise action, not to grade the business.

A "perfect" score is not the goal. A score of 7–8 with no risk flags is usually better than a 10 with a fake name.

---

## Profile accuracy — /10

How well does the GBP listing match the real-world business?

| Score | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Name, address, phone, website, and hours all match the real business exactly. No extra keywords. Special hours set. |
| 7–8  | Core info is correct. Minor issue like missing special hours or a secondary phone number that does not match the website. |
| 5–6  | One piece of core info is wrong or missing — e.g. wrong phone number, old address. |
| 3–4  | Multiple pieces of core info are wrong. |
| 1–2  | Nearly all info is wrong or the profile looks abandoned. |
| 0     | Profile has keyword-stuffed name, fake address, or is a duplicate. |

---

## Profile completeness — /10

How thoroughly has the GBP profile been filled in?

| Score | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | All sections filled: description, services/products, attributes, Q&A, posts, photos. Everything that applies is used. |
| 7–8  | Most sections filled. Missing one or two less critical things (e.g. no recent posts, Q&A not populated). |
| 5–6  | Basics done (name, address, phone, hours, category) but description, services, attributes are empty. |
| 3–4  | Only name, address, phone and hours are set. No photos, no description. |
| 1–2  | Bare bones. Category might be wrong. Essentials missing. |
| 0     | Profile exists but is practically empty. |

---

## Review system — /10

How well is the business managing reviews and reputation?

| Score | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Healthy review volume. Replies to both positive and negative reviews within days. Has a simple review-request process. No suspicious patterns. |
| 7–8  | Decent review volume. Replies present but inconsistent. Review-request process exists but could be better. |
| 5–6  | Some reviews but no replies. No review-request process. |
| 3–4  | Very few reviews. Negative reviews ignored. No replies at all. |
| 1–2  | Reviews suggest serious customer service problems. Negative reviews are met with argumentative replies. |
| 0     | Suspicious review pattern detected (spikes, identical language, incentivised reviews). |

---

## Mobile customer journey — /10

Can a customer complete the main action easily on a phone?

| Score | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Core action (call, book, order) is obvious within 3 seconds. Click-to-call works. Forms work. Site loads quickly on 4G. |
| 7–8  | Core action is clear but takes a tap or two more than ideal. Site is usable on mobile. |
| 5–6  | Core action is present but buried. Site works on mobile but feels clunky. |
| 3–4  | Core action is hard to find. Site is not mobile-friendly. |
| 1–2  | Site is barely usable on mobile. No click-to-call. Forms are broken. |
| 0     | No website, or website is completely broken. |

---

## Enquiry / contact flow — /10

What happens after a customer tries to get in touch?

| Score | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Contact form confirms submission. Follow-up email or text is timely and useful. Missed calls get a call back or text. Response time is clear. |
| 7–8  | Contact works but follow-up is basic or inconsistent. Missed call process exists but could be tighter. |
| 5–6  | Contact form works but no confirmation. No follow-up. Voicemail exists but calls are not consistently returned. |
| 3–4  | Contact form is broken or missing. No voicemail. Calls go unanswered. |
| 1–2  | Multiple contact methods are broken. No clear way to reach the business. |
| 0     | No contact method works. Phone disconnected. Website gone. |

---

## Trust signals — /10

Does the business look trustworthy to a new customer?

| Score | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| 9–10 | Real photos. Genuine reviews. Trade body logos or certifications visible. About page tells the story. Consistent branding. |
| 7–8  | Good reputation but not much effort to show it. Photos are genuine but could be better. |
| 5–6  | Photos are stock or very old. No about page. Sparse information. |
| 3–4  | Little information. No photos. Website feels generic or template-only. |
| 1–2  | Information is inconsistent. Website looks abandoned or suspicious. |
| 0     | Actively misleading information. Fake photos. Suspicious reviews. |

---

## Risk flags — Low / Medium / High

| Level | What it means |
|-------|---------------|
| **Low** | No risk flags found. Profile follows Google's guidelines. No concerning patterns. |
| **Medium** | Minor risk factors present — e.g. inconsistent NAP across directories, no review replies to negative reviews, outdated info. Fixable without risk of suspension. |
| **High** | Serious risk present — keyword-stuffed name, duplicate profiles, suspicious reviews, ineligible business type, fake address. Could lead to suspension. Needs immediate attention. |

---

## How to use the scores

1. Score each category based on the supplied evidence.
2. If evidence is missing, score based on what is known and note what is unchecked.
3. Use the scores to prioritise the action plan — focus on the lowest-scoring areas with the highest practical impact.
4. Include the scores in the Internal Audit Notes only. The client-facing report should use plain-language priorities, not number grades.
5. Never present scores as scientific, official, or Google-endorsed. They are a practical tool.
